# Story

Date line `<!-- YYYY-MM-DD story -->`.

A narrated story that teaches the topic through real historical events. The listener
follows people, and learns the idea at the moment the people in the story meet it.

| speaker | role | voice en / es |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| HOST_A | the narrator | af_bella / ef_dora |
| HOST_B to HOST_E | people in the story, for short lines of speech | see the panel table |

- The narrator has sixty to one hundred percent of the words. The lint checks it. Other
  voices are optional; use them for short lines that a person in the story says. In
  Spanish there are only two other voices, HOST_B and HOST_C.
- Plan one hundred ninety five words a minute (measured on a short test episode).
- The narrator may hold many turns in a row: each turn is one paragraph, and the
  renderer leaves a longer pause between them.
- **Facts first.** Before writing, list the real events, people, places, dates and
  quotes the story uses, each with its source, in a facts file beside the script (see
  "Facts and history" in the skill). Use only what is on that list as fact.
- **Say what is invented.** Scenes, thoughts and dialogue that nobody recorded are
  allowed, but the narrator says so once, early, in plain words: "the events are real;
  the words they say here are imagined". A real quote is introduced as one ("he later
  wrote:"). Never put an invented sentence in a real person's mouth as if it were a quote.
- **The shape.**
  1. Open in a scene, at a moment of trouble, not with a date. Within the first minute
     the narrator says what the episode will teach, in three things.
  2. Each of the three things is a turn of the story where the people meet the idea:
     the problem, the attempt, what they learn. Pause the story to explain the idea in
     plain words, then go back to the scene.
  3. End with what happened next in real life, then a recap of the three things, then a
     one-sentence sign-off.
- Lines by other voices are short, one or two sentences. The narrator says who speaks
  before or after ("the engineer shook his head."), since the listener cannot see faces.
- Keep the names of people few: two or three. Name each by role as well ("the young
  engineer, Maria").
