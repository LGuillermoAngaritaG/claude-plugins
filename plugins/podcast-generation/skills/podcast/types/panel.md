# Explainer panel

Date line `<!-- YYYY-MM-DD panel -->`.

A host and a panel of experts. Each expert owns one part of the topic and explains it
from their own field. The host runs the panel.

| speaker | role | voice en | voice es |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| HOST_A | the host | af_bella | ef_dora |
| HOST_B | expert one | am_michael | em_alex |
| HOST_C | expert two | bf_emma | em_santa |
| HOST_D | expert three | am_fenrir | none |
| HOST_E | expert four | bm_george | none |

- **Size.** In English, four or five voices: the host and three or four experts. In
  Spanish, three voices: the host and two experts, because Kokoro has only three Spanish
  voices. The lint refuses a fourth Spanish voice.
- A Spanish panel measured one hundred eighty five words a minute on a short test, not
  one hundred ninety five; plan for that until a longer episode says otherwise.
- The host has ten to thirty percent of the words. The lint checks it.
- Never two turns in a row by the same speaker.
- **Give each expert a field and a part.** For example, for coffee: a farmer on the
  beans, a chemist on the roast, a barista on the cup. Name each expert by their field,
  never by a made-up personal name, and say the field every time the host hands over
  ("our chemist, what happens in the roaster?"). The listener only has the voice and
  that word to tell them apart.
- **The opening.** The host welcomes, names the topic, and introduces each expert in one
  short line with their part. With four experts the opener names four parts, not three.
- **The body.** One section a part, in the order of the opening. Each section: the host
  asks, the expert explains in short turns, the host asks one follow-up. Every section
  also has one cross-talk moment: another expert adds a link from their own field ("from
  the farm side, that is why..."). This is what a panel gives that one voice cannot.
- Experts may disagree politely; the host names the disagreement and moves on.
- Keep each part about the same length, so no expert vanishes.
- **The close.** The host sums up one sentence a part, then asks every expert for one
  last line in the same order, then signs off.
