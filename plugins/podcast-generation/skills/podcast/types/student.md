# Student teaches expert

Date line `<!-- YYYY-MM-DD student -->`.

A student explains the topic as they understand it. An expert listens, completes the
picture, adds depth and fixes what is wrong. The listener learns next to the student.

| speaker | role | voice en / es |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| HOST_A | the student, who teaches | af_bella / ef_dora |
| HOST_B | the expert, who completes | am_michael / em_alex |

- The student has fifty five to eighty percent of the words (aim for sixty five). The
  lint checks it.
- Never two turns in a row by the same speaker.
- The student opens: a welcome, "I will try to teach this to someone who knows it far
  better than me", and the three things.
- The expert's turns are short and do one job each: a question that makes the student go
  further ("and why does that happen?"), a missing piece ("one thing to add"), or a fix.
- **Mistakes.** The student may get something wrong, at most once a section, and only a
  mistake a real learner makes. The expert fixes it in the very next turn, says plainly
  what the right version is and why the wrong one is tempting. The student then says the
  right version once, in their own words. The recap uses only the right version.
- The expert never lectures for more than about sixty words. If they need more, the
  student asks a question in between.
- The student gives the recap, the expert adds one last point, and the student signs off.
- The mid-sentence handoff works well here the other way round: the expert starts a
  sentence and the student finishes it, which shows they understood.
