# The script review

You review the narration of an explainer before anything is made from it: before the voice, the pictures and the
scenes. You did not write it. A problem found here costs one edit; found after the build it costs a new voice take,
new timing and new scene code. You get `script.json` (the narration, scene by scene; `<...>` tags are voice
directions, not words) and `facts.json` (every claim with its source). Read the whole script twice: once as a curious
adult who knows nothing of the field, once as a specialist looking for mistakes.

Report each problem as: scene, line number, what is wrong, and the exact new wording. Only real problems, most
serious first. Say "no problems" for a section that passes. Do not praise.

## Truth

1. A fact or number that no claim of `facts.json` covers, or a claim stronger than its source: "far less" where the
   source says "less", "most" where it says "many", "in turbines" where the source measured one turbine.
2. A definition or rule that is only true in part: "a turbine is any machine turned by water" (steam and wind
   turbines exist), a general statement drawn from one case.
3. Two scenes that disagree: one says the water leaves slower, a later one says the fastest water is where it leaves.

## Understanding

4. A word a curious adult would not know, used without a plain meaning where it is first said: name each one.
5. A term used before it is explained ("pumped storage" fifteen seconds before the lakes are described).
6. One word for two things, or two words for one thing: "outlet" for where the water comes in and later for where it
   leaves; "wheel", "impeller" and "runner" for the same part without saying so.
7. A step skipped: the viewer must know something the script has not said yet.

## Voice

8. Words the voice tends to blur or say as another word: short words at the end of a line, "real" (heard as
   "wheel"), a name spelled for its sound ("Oiler") that the captions would show spelled wrong.
9. A line so long it cannot be said in one breath, or a sentence the ear cannot parse.

## Attention

10. The first line: does it say what the video is about, and surprise?
11. Rhythm: a run of sentences of the same length; "and then" where "but" or "so" would carry the turn.
12. A "tone" that does not fit its lines (wonder on a warning); more than one voice tag in a scene.
13. The end: does the last line answer the opening question, without a summary list?
14. Each Short run (if the script marks them): does it start on a line that works as a hook without the scenes before
    it, and end on a payoff?
